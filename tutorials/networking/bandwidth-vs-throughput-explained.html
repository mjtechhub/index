Bandwidth vs Throughput Explained

While often used interchangeably, bandwidth and throughput are fundamentally different concepts in networking.

Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the theoretical or configured maximum capacity of a network link. It is the size of the "pipe." If you purchase a 1 Gbps internet connection, your bandwidth is 1 Gigabit per second.

Throughput

Throughput is the actual rate of useful data transferred over a period of time. A 1 Gbps link does not automatically mean you get 1 Gbps application download performance. Throughput is almost always lower than bandwidth.

Goodput

Goodput is the actual application-level payload delivered, excluding all protocol overhead (like Ethernet, IP, and TCP headers) and any packet retransmissions.

Factors Influencing Throughput

  • Protocol Overhead: IP and TCP headers consume bytes that aren't application data. VPN overhead consumes even more.
  • Latency and TCP Behavior: High latency can limit TCP throughput due to window sizing mechanics.
  • Packet Loss: Dropped packets require retransmissions, severely reducing goodput.
  • Hardware Limits: Server CPU limits, slow storage drives, or congested Wi-Fi environments.

Bits vs. Bytes

Network speeds are measured in bits (lowercase 'b'), while file sizes are measured in Bytes (uppercase 'B'). Because 8 bits = 1 Byte, a theoretical 100 Mbps connection maxes out at 12.5 MB/s (100 / 8 = 12.5) before any overhead or limitations are applied.
