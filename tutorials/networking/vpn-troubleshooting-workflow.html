VPN Troubleshooting Workflow

Troubleshooting VPNs requires isolating the failure domain: is the tunnel failing to build, or is traffic failing to traverse the built tunnel?

1. Identify the Symptom

  • Tunnel does not establish at all.
  • Tunnel establishes, but no traffic flows.
  • VPN connects, but local Internet breaks (common Remote Access split-tunnel issue).
  • Only specific subnets work (common Site-to-Site selector issue).

2. Verify Reachability

Can the client or peer router reach the external public IP of the VPN gateway? Check basic routing and ISP connectivity.

3. Phase 1: Authentication & Negotiation

If the tunnel fails to build, check IKE/IPsec or TLS session status. For Site-to-Site, this is almost always a proposal mismatch (different encryption/hash algorithms configured on each side), an incorrect peer IP, or a mismatched pre-shared key/certificate. For Remote Access, verify user credentials, MFA prompts, and endpoint posture checks.

4. Phase 2: Selectors and Subnets

If the tunnel builds but traffic fails, verify the "selectors" (the interesting traffic). In policy-based Site-to-Site VPNs, the local subnet configuration on Router A must perfectly mirror the remote subnet configuration on Router B. Overlapping subnets (where both sides use 192.168.1.0/24) will cause immediate routing failures.

5. Routing and NAT

Does the firewall know to route the internal traffic into the tunnel? Is NAT (Network Address Translation) improperly translating the private IPs before they enter the tunnel? A "NAT Exemption" rule is often required to prevent this.

6. Firewall Policy and MTU

VPN traffic is usually subject to stateful firewall inspection. Ensure a policy permits the decrypted traffic. Finally, if small pings work but file transfers fail, you likely have an MTU/MSS fragmentation issue due to the VPN encapsulation overhead.
