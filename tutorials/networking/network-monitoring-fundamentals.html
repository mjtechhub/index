Network Monitoring Fundamentals

Network monitoring is the practice of continuously observing a network to detect failing or slow components. It matters because proactive visibility minimizes downtime and degrades gracefully instead of failing catastrophically.

What to Monitor

  • Availability: Is the device online? Are specific service checks (like HTTP) responding?
  • Performance: What is the latency or packet loss across critical paths?
  • Capacity: Are interfaces approaching bandwidth limits? Are device CPU, memory, or hardware sensors (temperature) exceeding thresholds?
  • Errors: Are interfaces silently dropping packets or recording CRC errors?

Monitoring Concepts

Visibility is achieved through combinations of active checks (synthetic ping/HTTP requests), polling (pulling stats via SNMP or APIs), and passive data (receiving Syslog events, SNMP traps, or flow telemetry like NetFlow).

Modern tools use this data to establish baselines (normal historical behavior), identify trending patterns, and trigger alerting when metrics cross defined thresholds.

Troubleshooting Context

An alert is evidence of a condition, not automatically proof of root cause. A high-CPU alert on a router might be normal during a routing table recalculation, or it might be a denial-of-service attack.
