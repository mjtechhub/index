Duplex Mismatch & Speed Negotiation

Understanding how devices negotiate physical link properties is critical for resolving low-level packet loss and performance issues.

Duplex Modes

  • Half Duplex: A device can send OR receive data at any given time, but not both simultaneously. Often associated with legacy hubs or highly congested Wi-Fi.
  • Full Duplex: A device can send AND receive data simultaneously. This is the standard for modern switched Ethernet.

Auto-Negotiation

Ethernet auto-negotiation allows two connected devices to agree on the best possible speed and duplex mode. In modern switched Ethernet networks, full-duplex with auto-negotiation enabled is typically preferred and expected.

The Duplex Mismatch

A classic duplex mismatch occurs when one side of a link is manually hardcoded (e.g., to 100/Full) while the other side is left to auto-negotiate. The auto-negotiating side can usually detect the speed, but cannot detect the duplex, falling back to a safe default of Half-Duplex.

Symptoms of a mismatch include:

  • Extremely poor throughput (while basic pings might work fine).
  • Collisions or late-collision counters incrementing on legacy or shared Ethernet interfaces.
  • Frame errors and heavy TCP retransmissions.
  • General performance instability.

Modern Accuracy

Duplex mismatches are significantly less common on modern infrastructure. Modern 1 Gbps and higher Ethernet standards mandate auto-negotiation to establish master/slave clocking correctly. While you can sometimes force speed/duplex on 1G interfaces depending on the vendor, disabling auto-negotiation on modern multi-gigabit links is generally discouraged and often unsupported.
