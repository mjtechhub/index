Packet Loss Troubleshooting

Packet loss occurs when one or more data packets traveling across a network fail to reach their destination.

Potential Causes

Packet loss is a symptom, not a root cause. Common culprits include:

  • Network congestion or overloaded device queues.
  • Bad physical cabling or faulty interfaces.
  • Wireless interference or weak signal.
  • Duplex or speed mismatches.
  • ISP or upstream provider issues.
  • Traffic policing or shaping policies dropping excess traffic.
  • Packets dropped by firewall or security policies.
  • MTU mismatch leading to unfragmentable packet drops.

Troubleshooting Workflow

  1. Determine Scope: Is it affecting one device, an entire VLAN, or all Internet-bound traffic?
  2. Test Local Gateway: Ping your default gateway to rule out local Wi-Fi/LAN issues. Compare wired vs. wireless behavior.
  3. Inspect Interfaces: Log into switches/routers and check interface counters for CRC errors, collisions, or input/output drops.
  4. Check Utilization: Check monitoring systems to see if a link is saturated (100% bandwidth usage).
  5. Use Path Tools: Use tracert / traceroute or pathping to identify where loss begins.

Critical Concept: Interpreting ICMP Loss

ICMP loss does not always equal application loss. Many routers intentionally rate-limit or deprioritize ICMP echo replies generated by their control plane to protect their CPUs. Do not interpret one intermediate-hop loss value (e.g., hop 4 shows 50% loss, but hop 5 and the final destination show 0% loss) as definitive end-to-end packet loss. If the final destination is not experiencing loss, the intermediate hop is merely ignoring the pings.
