Unicast vs Broadcast vs Multicast

Learn about Unicast vs Broadcast vs Multicast in this Networking tutorial.

Traffic on a network fundamentally flows in one of three ways depending on the destination intent.

1. Unicast

Unicast is one-to-one communication. A single sender transmits data to a single specific destination.

  • Example: Your PC loading a web page from a specific Web Server. Most internet traffic is unicast.

2. Broadcast

Broadcast is one-to-all communication. A single sender transmits data to every device within the local broadcast domain (typically synonymous with a VLAN).

  • Example: An ARP request where a PC asks the entire network, "Who has IP 192.168.1.1?"
  • Important Distinction: IPv6 does NOT use broadcast traffic. IPv6 relies entirely on targeted multicast to achieve similar goals.

3. Multicast

Multicast is one-to-many communication. A single sender transmits one copy of the data, and the network hardware replicates it only to the specific group of devices that have explicitly requested to receive it.

  • Example: A live video stream being sent to hundreds of employees. Sending unicast would overwhelm the server; sending broadcast would overwhelm the entire network. Multicast efficiently sends one stream that is duplicated at the edge switches.
  • Management: IPv4 relies conceptually on IGMP (Internet Group Management Protocol) for clients to join a multicast group. IPv6 relies on MLD (Multicast Listener Discovery).
